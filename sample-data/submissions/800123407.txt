Should Cities Ban Cars Downtown?

Mid-sized cities should ban cars from their downtown cores, but only after investing in transit, because a ban without alternatives would punish the workers who keep downtown running.

The benefits of a car-free core are well documented. Pontevedra, Spain eliminated traffic deaths in its center after removing cars, and Oslo cleared parking from its core in 2019 and saw pedestrian traffic rise. Streets without cars can host seating, trees, and outdoor dining that make people want to stay.

But who works downtown? Restaurant cooks, janitors, and retail staff often work late shifts, after buses stop running. A 2019 Brookings Institution analysis found that low-wage workers in mid-sized metros are the least likely to live near frequent transit. For them, a sudden ban would mean longer commutes or lost jobs.

Some argue that a ban itself will force cities to improve transit. History suggests otherwise: service follows funding, not policy statements. Cities should first run frequent, late-night buses and build edge parking, then phase in the ban.

A car-free downtown is a good goal. Getting there fairly is the part that matters.
